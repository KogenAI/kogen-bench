# F12 — Explicit lever and recipe arms

**Status: INTERIM.** A flag or recipe name is not evidence that a distinct arm ran. Historical screens often bundle changes, use different task/host cohorts, or lack a cell-to-arm join. The register below records what would falsify each proposition and whether a separate, attributable comparison is established in the public record.

## Hypotheses

- **H117 — Planner context pack.** A compact context pack may produce a better or cheaper plan than a file-list input. Compare the two inputs with all other planner and builder settings fixed.
- **H118 — Plan guard.** A plan sanity/constraint gate may prevent plans that would otherwise cause Build failures. Compare downstream outcomes and gate cost with and without the guard.
- **H119 — Plan examples.** Relevant examples may improve transfer from plan to implementation. Hold the plan and builder settings fixed while changing example exposure.
- **H120 — Plan reproduction evidence.** Evidence that reproduces a failure may improve the plan's diagnosis and implementation. Compare with an otherwise identical plan without that evidence.
- **H121 — Plan interfaces.** Explicit cross-component interfaces may reduce integration failures. Compare matched tasks with interfaces present or absent.
- **H122 — Planner turns.** Varying the number of planner turns may change completion enough to justify its time and token cost. Compare fixed turn budgets.
- **H123 — Plan debate.** Competing plans may improve plan choice and downstream task success. Compare debate against a single-plan control.
- **H124 — Plan specificity.** Criteria, approach, and step-level plans may affect builders differently. Compare each level within builder and task strata.
- **H125 — Review scope.** A narrower review surface may reduce cost while preserving useful findings. Compare findings, false blocks, missed defects, and review cost.
- **H126 — Executable review.** Running review checks may detect defects that text-only review misses. Measure true and false review findings and prospective repair outcomes.
- **H127 — Contract gate.** Checking requirements before acceptance or escalation may catch incomplete work. Compare detection and false-block rates, then measure Build outcomes after any repair.
- **H128 — Best-of-N.** Generating and selecting among candidates may increase successful completion enough to justify the additional attempts. Compare selected outcomes with the same direct baseline and report pass@N separately.
- **H129 — Hybrid pipeline.** Combining staged modes may improve the pass/cost frontier over its constituent recipes. Compare the complete bundle and every constituent arm.
- **H130 — Split work.** Dividing implementation among workers may increase successful throughput. Compare a fixed split plan with a solo arm, including coordination cost and wall time.
- **H131 — Routing policies.** Dynamic, forced, or versioned routing may assign useful models more effectively than fixed routes. Compare on the same tasks and model/effort choices.
- **H132 — Route cascade.** Triggering escalation only after failure may preserve quality at lower cost than always escalating. Compare policy-level success and cost against fixed direct models.
- **H133 — Cascade gate.** Contract, review, or combined gates may differ in their ability to trigger useful escalation. Compare gate sensitivity, false triggers, success, and cost.
- **H134 — Context handoff.** Passing prepared context to later stages may reduce repeated work or cost without reducing completion. Compare a handoff arm with a matched no-handoff arm.
- **H135 — Recipe attribution.** Staged, direct, direct-shell, direct-escalate, plan-shell, and escalate-shell recipes may differ in outcome and cost. Each needs an explicit arm manifest and direct within-cohort comparison.
- **H136 — Ladder composition.** Luna-only, Sol-effort, and diverse ladders may differ in success and cost. Compare each ladder under a fixed task set and trigger rule.
- **H137 — Harness-version effect.** Changes in Codex CLI or Claude Code versions may change earlier harness comparisons. Compare versions on identical tasks, requests, and host conditions.
- **H138 — Matched model/effort baselines.** Kogen configurations with Luna max or Sol low/medium/high builders, with and without Sol planning, may differ from direct baselines. Match the effective model and effort at each tested role.
- **H139 — Raw versus shaped Intent.** Supplying a raw external-project Intent instead of a provided shaped Intent may change Kogen's apparent advantage. Compare both inputs against the same task and model baselines.

## Design implications

- Require a frozen recipe manifest with each lever's exact flag, model role, prompt/config hash, task IDs, effective effort, revision, and stop behavior.
- Use a one-factor contrast when estimating a lever; register bundle and interaction tests separately.
- Give claim-bearing model, routing, and speed comparisons matched tasks, effective settings, hosts, and epochs, with exact cell IDs and complete ITT disposition.
- Keep developer-set tuning rounds separate from held-out evidence and report selection history.

## Explicit non-implications

- A named command-line flag does not prove that the flag was separately tested or caused an outcome.
- A bundle that works on one selected task does not establish a transferable recipe.
- An offline reviewer or selector diagnostic does not show that a live Build improves.
- A screen that does not show a lift does not prove a model ceiling or equivalence.
- R72, R74, and hill-climb iteration results are not interchangeable ladder observations.

## Smallest open tests

- Freeze a public recipe manifest for each candidate lever and map it to immutable cell IDs before grading.
- Run one registered one-factor comparison per arm on the same task set, with direct and bundle controls, matched effective model/effort, and full request, cost, wall, and ITT records.
- For routing and cascade gates, compare policies on a held-out cohort and include threshold calibration, false-trigger rates, and all helper costs.
- For shell or split claims, measure correctness, test coverage, requests, and wall on matched tasks; do not substitute task throughput for completion quality.
