# F07 Tasks, grading, and method

This page records evidence for H75–H90. These propositions concern task selection, grading, study design, and reproducibility. A methodological recommendation is not itself a measured Build effect.

## Hypotheses and current status

| ID | Falsifiable proposition | Current evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| H75 | A cheap starting task and one-lever-at-a-time climb identifies useful task and recipe changes without mistaking saturation for general success. | INTERIM. Several Studio hill-climb iterations are reported, but cell-level outcomes and the final iteration are not fully reconciled in the public export. |
| H76 | Luna or Grok is the better low-cost, fast starting builder for the intended task set. | NOT ESTABLISHED. No matched comparison for this starting-model choice is linked to a public claim. |
| H77 | A benchmark limited to Rails tasks can misstate performance on Elixir/Phoenix or Rust work. | NOT ESTABLISHED. Cross-stack task and implementation records exist, but no matched task-set study isolates stack or recency effects. |
| H78 | Public real-codebase tasks pinned to source revisions are more reproducible than hidden replay tasks. | NOT ESTABLISHED. Public task provenance is useful for reproduction, but no task-quality comparison tests the proposition. |
| H79 | SQLite tasks allow more reliable parallel execution than PostgreSQL-heavy tasks while a smaller PostgreSQL set preserves realism. | NOT ESTABLISHED. No controlled database-task concurrency comparison is published. |

## Design implications

Task difficulty, stack, host, provider epoch, and implementation revision can each change what a comparison means. Current records justify keeping task sets, guard cells, smokes, stopped slots, official grades, and counterfactual outcomes separate. They do not validate a single adaptive allocation policy or a broad Kogen advantage claim.

## What this evidence does not show

The hill-climb summaries do not establish that one lever caused its observed outcome or that its apparent benefit transfers beyond the selected tasks. A cross-stack cohort does not establish task-set representativeness. Prepared or stopped cells are not scored results. Reporting completeness is not evidence that the recorded comparison was balanced.

## Smallest open tests

Publish exact, immutable task/arm/cell joins and reconcile invalid cases before estimating ITT. For H75 and H83–H85, preregister an allocation rule and evaluate both decision quality and resource use against a fixed-allocation baseline. For H77–H79, balance source project, stack, database, task difficulty, and model protocol. For H80–H89, audit controls, registration order, host and provider epochs, revision pins, and stop dispositions against the raw records.
